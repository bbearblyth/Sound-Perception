1. Double-flash illusion

Temporal ventriloquism: one brief flash heard with two quick beeps is often seen as two flashes (Shams, Kamitani & Shimojo, 2000).

Ask: "How many flashes did you see?" Run a few mystery trials and count the hands. Then reveal.

2. Stream or bounce?

Two discs glide through each other. Silent, most people see them pass through. A click at the moment they overlap makes most people see them bounce (Sekuler, Sekuler & Lau, 1997).

Ask: "Did they pass through or bounce off?" Compare the three conditions. The early click should weaken the bounce, because timing is what binds the click to the collision.

Sound is panned to the centre

3. Spatial ventriloquism

The sound never moves from the centre, but a dot pulsing in time with it drags the sound towards the dot. Vision is sharper in space, so the brain trusts it (Alais & Burr, 2004).

Ask: "Point to where the sound is coming from." Move the dot from side to side, then switch to out of sync. The pull should drop once sound and pulse stop lining up.
